## 2. Verdict

**FAIL.** The area is close: the ownership register, the CNPG generation scheme, the stable-named Longhorn triplets, the bucket and credential split, and the retention register are well thought out. Two blockers stop sign-off. (1) A seed-layer Job depends on an image that only exists after Forgejo and trusted CI are running, which deadlocks P04 and black start. (2) A supported, default-for-some-providers credential mode puts the account-wide object-storage admin key into the cluster, which breaks the backup-protection model the plans rely on. The disk budget also contradicts the plans' own eviction and alert thresholds. Each fix is bounded and listed below.

| SPEC §125 DoD item | Sign off? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| durable-state ownership is defined | **No (yes after STO-5, STO-14, STO-20)** | The plan 11 register is thorough and almost every item has exactly one system of record. Three gaps remain. `hermes-data` is owned twice: the chart creates the PVC (plans 24, 54), and GitOps also declares a stable-named triplet for it (plans 13, ADR-0203). Plan 11 sits on no DAG node, so none of its ACs, including the coverage and disk ACs, belongs to a gate. Protection claims in the register contradict plan 14. |
| backup destinations are defined | **No (yes after STO-2, STO-12)** | The destinations are defined: ten buckets, GitHub, operator media. But in `S3_CREDENTIAL_MODE=shared` the cluster holds the `admin` key, so it can permanently destroy `tofu-state`, `forgejo-objects`, `evidence` and `bootstrap-seed`, and rewrite lifecycle on locked buckets. This contradicts ADR-0204 and plan 14 §1. Consumer-client compatibility with Object Lock buckets is first tested only after the lock decision has become irreversible. |
| retention/GC policy is defined | **No (yes after STO-3, STO-4, STO-8)** | The retention register covers every SPEC §107 item, with mechanisms, checks and alerts. "Root disks SHALL not fill" does not hold, though. The year-one disk budget allocates 95 % of the disk, while the warning fires at 75 %, soft eviction starts at 85 % and Longhorn stops scheduling at 85 %. On-node restore tests need 2× the source size free, which the budget does not provide. No per-node disk budget exists for small-ha. The `ci-workspace` mapping can silently fall back to the root filesystem. |

## 5. Items for `plans/spec-issues.md`

1. **SPEC §11/§107/§109 — false assumption / cost-risk (missing; plan 11 Q-11-2 cites "SI-G3-01", which is absent from the register).** On the single node, a 100 GiB disk cannot hold one year of growth below the 75 % warning, 85 % eviction and 85 % Longhorn-minimum thresholds, *and* the 2× scratch space that on-node restore tests of Longhorn and CNPG need. 75 GiB is worse. Resolution options: raise the minimum or preferred disk; or run heavy restore tests off-node (drill installation or streamed verification); or accept a shorter growth horizon with a measured gate. Blocking: no once STO-3 is fixed, but an operator decision is needed before 75 GiB is advertised.
2. **SPEC §12 (small-ha 75 GiB minimum per node) — false assumption.** The per-node worst case at small-ha (STO-4) likely exceeds 75 GiB, and possibly 100 GiB. Needs a measured budget and possibly an amendment.
3. **SPEC §29/§114 vs single-key S3 providers — false assumption (extends SI-17).** "The cluster SHALL not be the sole failure domain for critical backup data" cannot be met when the provider offers only one account-wide key and the cluster must hold it, unless every system-of-record bucket uses COMPLIANCE lock or the off-cluster records (`tofu-state`, `recovery`) live in a second account. Record the resolution chosen under STO-2.
4. **SPEC §108 project-repository RPO for large repositories** — only if STO-13 is resolved by accepting the 6 h incremental cadence. That is a weakening and must be recorded as such.
